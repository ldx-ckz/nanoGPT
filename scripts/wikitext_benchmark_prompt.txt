A public library is both a collection of books and a place where people learn
how to use information. Its rooms may hold printed maps, local newspapers,
recordings, photographs, and documents that describe the history of a town.
Some visitors come to find a particular fact. Others come with a question that
changes as they discover new sources.

Consider a student who wants to understand how a railway influenced the growth
of a neighborhood. A map from before the railway was built can show where
streets and buildings originally stood. A timetable can show which journeys
became possible when the station opened. Newspaper advertisements can offer
clues about how local businesses responded. Each source answers a different
part of the question, and each has limits. A map can show the location of a
factory, for example, but rarely tells us how the people who worked there
traveled to work.

To build an explanation, the student compares dates, checks the names of
streets, and looks for evidence that two records describe the same place.
